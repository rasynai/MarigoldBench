**Abstract (lines 168–184).** Motivation: LLMs exceed 90% on MMLU-class benchmarks, limiting measurement. HLE = 2,500 questions, dozens of subjects, multi-modal, multiple-choice + short-answer, automated grading. "Each question has a known solution that is unambiguous and easily verifiable, but cannot be quickly answered via internet retrieval." SOTA LLMs show low accuracy and low calibration. Multi-modal (text-only or text+image); multiple-choice and exact-match for automatic verification. Emphasizes world-class mathematics. ## Benchmark anatomy

- **n items:** 2,500 public questions (+ unreported-size private held-out set, + a second private set from post-release late contributions). Funnel: 70,000 logged pre-submission attempts → ~13,000 LLM-stumping submissions → 6,000 candidates after round-1 review → 2,500 public. Formats: 76% exact-match, 24% multiple-choice (5+ options); ~14% require an image. Subject mix: Math 41%, Bio/Med 11%, CS/AI 10%, Physics 9%, Humanities/SS 9%, Other 9%, Chemistry 7%, Engineering 4%; 100+ self-declared subjects.
- **Construction:** crowd-sourced from ~1,000 expert contributors (500+ institutions, 50 countries; mostly professors/researchers/graduate degree holders), incentivized by a $500,000 prize pool ($5,000 x 50, $500 x 500) and co-authorship. Original or non-trivially synthesized questions; unpublished research knowledge explicitly welcomed.
- **Validation/review:** (1) automated pre-submission gate — item must stump a fixed frontier panel (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, o1; +o1-mini, o1-preview for text-only); exact-match must stump all, MC all but one; (2) round-1 human review, 1–3 graduate-level reviews per item with iterative refinement (3–5 min/review; full solution verification waived if >5 min); (3) round-2 approval by organizers + trained top reviewers with anti-"artificial difficulty" rubric; (4) post-release: bug bounty, Scale AI student audits (solve-to-consensus), searchability audit via search-on/search-off model comparison.
- **Human baseline:** none measured. No human test-takers sat the exam; correctness rests on author-provided solutions plus review. The empirical stand-in is the expert disagreement rate: 15.4% (public set, two 200-question audit rounds with author rebuttal), ~18% on a bio/chem/health subset, 25% under a single-dissenting-reviewer rule (vs FutureHouse's external ~30% claim).
- **Contamination defenses:** originality requirement + non-searchability requirement enforced at review; searchable-question purge using search-enabled models; private held-out split(s) explicitly for overfitting detection; HLE-Rolling dynamic fork for post-saturation migration. No canary string or licensing defense is mentioned.
- **Scoring:** binary accuracy, graded by an LLM judge (o3-mini-2025-01-31, structured decoding: extracted_final_answer/reasoning/correct/confidence) matching against the reference answer with format-equivalence and "small margin of error" tolerance; GPT-4o judge used in the earlier submission pipeline. No partial credit.
- **Judge design:** judge instructed to only compare answers, not solve; 'None' extraction if no final answer; no judge-vs-human agreement rate reported anywhere.
- **Statistical reporting:** none beyond point estimates — no confidence intervals, no standard errors, no significance tests, no clustering, single run per model (temperature 0.0 where configurable; o1/o3-mini forced to 1.0; Gemini 2.0 FT at 0.7). Calibration reported as RMS calibration error from verbalized 0–100% confidence.

## Limitations

**Admitted by the authors:**
- Low model scores are "partially by design" via adversarial filtering; noise-driven non-zero floor; "small inflections close to zero accuracy are not strongly indicative of progress" — the capability floor is unknowable.
- Review was shallow by budget: reviewers were not expected to verify solutions taking >5 minutes; error correction was deferred to post-release bug bounty and audits.
- Nonzero label noise: 15.4% expert disagreement on the public set, ~18% in bio/chem/health; some MC questions only have a "most plausible" answer, not a verified one; research-experience questions are hard for anyone to verify.
- Closed-ended academic knowledge only — success would not imply autonomous research ability or AGI; expected to saturate quickly (>50% by end-2025 deemed plausible), hence HLE-Rolling.
- Tool-free evaluation makes calculator/code-requiring questions "artificially difficult," which round 2 tried to filter.

**Not admitted (my observations):**
- Zero statistical machinery: no CIs, no SEs, no tests, single runs, mixed temperatures (0.0 vs forced 1.0 vs 0.7) — at n=2,500 a 95% CI on 5% accuracy is roughly ±0.9pp, so many adjacent Table 1/2/3 gaps (e.g., 2.7 vs 3.0) are not distinguishable; category cells (e.g., Chemistry ~170 items) have CIs of several points. The paper never notes this.
- The measured signal (2.7–13.4%) is at or below the label-noise floor (15.4–18% disagreement); the paper reports both numbers but never confronts that model "accuracy" differences may be dominated by mislabeled or ambiguous items.
- No measured human baseline whatsoever — "expert human frontier" performance is asserted by construction (authors know their own answers), not demonstrated; no single human could take this exam, and no expert panel accuracy is reported.
- Panel-relative difficulty: filtering against a specific 2024/early-2025 model panel selects for those models' idiosyncratic blind spots; later models' gains partly reflect not sharing the panel's quirks rather than crossing the "frontier of human knowledge." Scores of the filter-panel models (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini 1.5, o1) are also biased downward by construction relative to non-panel models — cross-model comparisons in Table 1 are structurally unfair.
- No judge validation: o3-mini judge equivalence-checking (including nontrivial symbolic algebra, as their own example shows) has an unreported error rate; judge errors at the few-percent level would be comparable to the entire score range. Also the judge changed between pipeline stages (GPT-4o at submission, o3-mini at evaluation).
- Incentive distortion: $5,000/question and authorship-by-acceptance reward stumping the panel, which favors adversarial obscurity and trivia; the round-2 rubric fights this ("artificially difficult," "not interesting") but with subjective criteria and no measurement.
- "Broad" coverage is 41% mathematics; humanities and several sciences are single-digit shares.
- Public release of all 2,500 items maximizes contamination risk for every post-January-2025 model; private-set size, composition, and any public-vs-private divergence results are never reported, so the advertised overfitting check has no published teeth.
- Iterative question modification until models fail (e.g., changing the number of MC options) means item difficulty is an artifact of an adversarial loop, not a stable construct.

## Implications for CRUCIBLE-CHAIN

1. **Power must come from independent templates, not instances: treat the template as the sampling/clustering unit.** HLE's 2,500 items are informative because they come from ~1,000 independent authors across 100+ subjects; CRUCIBLE-CHAIN's 8 templates x ~18 instances is effectively n≈8 for any generalization claim, since instances within a template share the recipe (and the recipe is what leaked). Concretely: report cluster-robust or hierarchical-bootstrap CIs over templates, and grow the template count (target 30–50 independent templates) before growing instances. Rough sizing under independence: separating 5% vs 10% pass rates at alpha=0.05, power 0.8 needs ~430 items/model; with within-template ICC of 0.2–0.5 at 18 instances/template the design effect is ~4.4–9.5, so at 8 templates no instance count suffices — the binding constraint is templates, exactly as HLE's binding constraint was authors.

2. **Adopt HLE's pre-submission difficulty gate, generator-side, with a frozen documented panel — and expect panel-relative decay.** HLE only accepted items that stumped a named panel (exact-match: all models; MC: all but one, a stricter-than-chance criterion), logging 70,000 attempts to get 13,000 qualifying items. CRUCIBLE-CHAIN should calibrate each template's judgment-call parameters (distractor gap, defect subtlety) against a frozen frontier panel until clean-condition pass rates hit the single-digit target, record the panel + versions (HLE's Table 4 is the model), and pre-register a recalibration trigger, because HLE's own authors forecast >50% within a year of release ("plausible that models could exceed 50% accuracy on HLE by the end of 2025") and stood up HLE-Rolling for that day. A generator makes rolling regeneration nearly free — schedule seed-rotation releases.

3. **Fix the leakage channel HLE never had: the prompt itself.** HLE's contamination defenses (originality, search-on/search-off audits, private split) target retrieval from the world; CRUCIBLE-CHAIN saturated because the prompt leaked method recipes and answer menus — an in-context leak no held-out split fixes. Actionable: strip all procedural scaffolding from task prompts; never enumerate candidate answers (HLE's own reviewers observed that models flip to correct when formatting/help is added: "Models often get questions right after LaTeX formatting is added or improved" — the same sensitivity works in reverse); prefer exact-match over multiple-choice everywhere (HLE is 76% exact-match and had to inflate MC option counts to fight lucky guessing); and additionally keep an HLE-style private seed split, publishing public-vs-private deltas per model as the overfitting probe HLE promised but never published numbers for.

4. **Constructed truth is the moat — protect and advertise it with an audit.** HLE's Achilles heel is label noise: 15.4% expert disagreement (18% in bio/chem/health, 25% single-reviewer, ~30% claimed by FutureHouse) against 2.7–13.4% model scores, so the signal sits below the noise floor, and post-hoc patching required a bug bounty, student audit teams, and a rolling fork. CRUCIBLE-CHAIN's deterministic generator gives near-zero label error — the exact property that makes a single-digit pass-rate target interpretable. But "chains of judgment calls" can reintroduce HLE-style ambiguity: for each stage, verify the intended call is uniquely derivable from the given data (property-based tests + an independent reimplementation of the generator), run a small human-expert audit (HLE used 2 x 200-item samples with author rebuttal — a cheap, replicable protocol) and publish the measured disagreement rate as a headline differentiator vs HLE.

5. **Score programmatically; if any extraction model is unavoidable, validate it — HLE didn't.** HLE relies on an o3-mini judge doing genuine symbolic equivalence reasoning (their worked example proves a trig identity) with no reported judge-human agreement, and it swapped judges between pipeline stages (GPT-4o → o3-mini). With near-zero-label-error tasks and single-digit targets, judge error would dominate CRUCIBLE-CHAIN's measurement. Use deterministic parsers with explicit numeric tolerance (HLE's "within a small margin of error" rule, but codified); if a model must extract free-text stage answers, copy HLE's narrow structured-extraction prompt (extract-and-compare only, judge forbidden from solving) and report extraction agreement against a hand-graded sample — a one-paragraph table HLE lacks that reviewers will ask for.

6. **Report per-stage and refusal-condition metrics the way HLE reports calibration — and make blanket refusal a losing strategy.** HLE's second headline (RMS calibration error >70%: models confabulate confidently rather than abstain) shows abstention-quality metrics get cited as much as accuracy. CRUCIBLE-CHAIN's flawed-premise condition is a sharper instrument, but it is gameable: a model that learns some tasks want refusal can refuse indiscriminately. Score conditions jointly per model — e.g., defect/premise-flag precision and recall alongside clean-condition pass rate, requiring high clean accuracy for flag credit — and additionally elicit verbalized confidence per stage (HLE's Explanation/Answer/Confidence format) to report a calibration number comparable to HLE's Table 1. Per-stage scoring also multiplies statistical power (5–8 Bernoulli observations per item instead of 1) without abandoning non-compensatory headline scoring.

7. **Steal the two human checks that survive automation, skip the rest.** HLE's heavy funnel (1–3 reviews/item at 3–5 min, round-2 elite approval, bug bounty, Scale student audits, searchability audit) exists because human-authored items fail in uncontrolled ways; a generator eliminates most of that. Keep: (a) an "attractive wrong path" empirical audit — HLE round 2 discards items that are "artificially difficult" or merely adversarial; CRUCIBLE-CHAIN's analog is verifying with baseline model runs that each stage's designed distractor is actually taken (a distractor nobody takes adds length, not difficulty, to the chain); (b) a public bug-bounty window on template logic itself, since a single generator bug corrupts ~18 instances at once — HLE's per-item error blast radius was 1, CRUCIBLE-CHAIN's is a full cluster, which is the flip side of constructed truth.
