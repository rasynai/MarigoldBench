# Hacker News: benchmark skepticism in model-launch discourse

**Venue:** news.ycombinator.com (comments on model launches, benchmark papers, and benchmark-scandal stories)
**Method:** Algolia HN search API (hn.algolia.com) — ~25 distinct comment/story queries ("Goodhart benchmark", "benchmark contamination", "trained on the test set", "benchmaxx", "benchmarks are meaningless", "held-out benchmark", "private benchmark suite", "benchmark saturated", "vibe check benchmark", "chart crime", "leaderboard illusion", etc.), followed by full-tree or story-scoped comment pulls from ~20 threads spanning Dec 2024 – Aug 2026 (plus older pre-LLM benchmark threads for context). All quotes below are verbatim from HN comments; each links to the comment permalink.
**Date compiled:** 2026-08-16

## 1. Distinct complaint patterns

### 1.1 Goodhart's law is the community's reflexive frame — recited on nearly every launch thread

The single most common benchmark comment on HN is some paraphrase of "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." It is deployed as a conversation-ending explanation for any suspicious score.

- jandrese on the Meta/LMArena story: "When a measure becomes a target it is no longer a good measure. These ML benchmarks were never going to last very long. There is far too much pressure to game them, even unintentionally." ([43625004](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43625004))
- mmaunder on the Putnam-variation result: "I know it's a cliche, but this really is Goodhart's Law at work with the benchmarks becoming targets." ([42567332](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42567332))
- lstodd takes it to the limit: "Benchmark is by definition gamed. That is the essence of Goodhart's law." ([49191087](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49191087))
- ttoinou on Hunyuan-T1: "What if this reinforcement is just gaming the benchmarks (Goodhart's law) without providing better answers elsewhere, how would we notice it?" ([43447986](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43447986))
- utopiah predicts the lifecycle in advance: "Goodhart's law will make this 'benchmark' moot in weeks if not days. It will get integrated back into the fold, it will look 'solved' but there will still be no reasoning." ([48308433](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48308433))
- An entire front-page essay, "Goodhart's Law Comes for Every Benchmark You Trust" (Jul 2026, [49126716](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49126716)), whose top comment is: "Jokes on them, I don't trust benchmarks. Once something becomes a benchmark it is no longer a good benchmark." (functionmouse, [49187971](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49187971))

### 1.2 "Trained on the test set" — contamination as the default explanation for any surprising score

When a model outperforms expectations, HN's first hypothesis is contamination, not capability. This is asserted both as informed suspicion and as documented fact.

- andrepd, on FrontierMath announcement: "How come e.g. o1 scores so high on these reasoning and math and IMO benchmarks and then fails every simple question I ask of it? The answer is training on the test set." ([42099452](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42099452))
- retinaros on the Putnam-variation result: "trained on test. who even trusts OAI anymore?" ([42565850](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565850))
- sdenton4 on Gemma 4: "there's a good chance that they have trained on the test set, making the eval scores useless... you can't meaningfully compare models which have trained on test to those which have not." ([47621390](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47621390))
- Documented catch: porridgeraisin relays how a German open model's headline score was traced to training "on 10 epochs of a 'very light rephrasing' of EVERY gpqa diamond EVAL data" ([48925912](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48925912)).
- vessenes spots contamination *from the vendor's own numbers*: "they definitely have some benchmark contamination: the blog post shows a substantial comparative drop in swebench verified vs open tests" ([45839406](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45839406)).
- disambiguation compresses the lifecycle: "the trend seems to be: today's benchmark becomes tomorrow's training data." ([42807492](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42807492))
- davidgerard on Humanity's Last Exam, day one: "The very first thing that will happen is every company training against this benchmark, as they do every other benchmark." ([42824260](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42824260))
- AIPedant notes labs' contamination checks are performatively weak: "It's like OpenAI using exact text search to identify benchmark contamination for the GPT-4 technical report. I am quite certain they had more sophisticated tools available." ([44534526](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44534526))

### 1.3 "Benchmaxxing" — a native HN vocabulary word for tuning models to look good on paper

By 2026 the community has a standing pejorative for it, applied heavily to small/open models but also to frontier labs.

- Aurornis on Qwen: "In the local LLM communities there is a lot of respect for the Qwen models, but everyone comes to acknowledge that they do a lot of benchmaxxing after using them. Even at full precision they're never as good as models with similar benchmarks." ([49301927](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49301927))
- EB66: "I see wide differences between what our internal benchmarks report and what the major benchmarks do. There was a whole lot of fanfare about how amazing GLM 5.2 was... but it was pure rubbish on our internal benchmark." ([49304365](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49304365))
- weitendorf claims you can *smell* it in reasoning traces: "you typically see smarter and less benchmaxxed models' thinking traces spending more time exploring the solution space, and benchmaxxed models more time trying to refine/decide on the response contents... trying to 'remember'/re-derive the answer rather than arrive at it deductively" ([49307486](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49307486)).
- eis notices the synchronized jumps: "Grok, Meta, Gemini and others all released updates... and made significant jumps in benchmarks all around the same time. Any guesses as to why that is? ... everyone is benchmaxxing?" ([49289487](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49289487))
- inigyou, maximal form: "All machine learning is benchmaxxing. It's a big problem." ([49309168](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49309168))
- blcknight (note: this flavor often carries a geopolitical edge): "Chinese models are almost certainly cheating on benchmarks, I would bet if you saw the training data that the benchmark canaries are in there." ([48719241](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48719241))

### 1.4 Leaderboard gaming as institutional scandal — Llama 4 / LMArena and "The Leaderboard Illusion"

The April 2025 Llama 4 episode (an unreleased "experimental chat version" of Maverick submitted to LMArena) and Cohere's "Leaderboard Illusion" paper crystallized the view that arena-style leaderboards are hill-climbed by insiders.

- croemer, in the Llama 4 launch thread: "Relevant update: the model on LM Arena is not the one that was released. See 'Meta got caught gaming AI benchmark'." ([43619248](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43619248))
- The divergence was quantified in-thread: anotherpaulg reports "Llama 4 Maverick scored 16% on the aider polyglot coding benchmark" while sitting #2 on LMArena ([43604919](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43604919)); cuuupid: "this exposes some serious flaws in benchmarks... there is either a gap between user preference and model performance, or between model performance and whatever benchmarks assess" ([43596277](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43596277)).
- Later confirmation circulated as its own story: "Yann LeCun confirms Meta's Llama 4 benchmarks were 'fudged a little bit'" ([46497615](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46497615)).
- On The Leaderboard Illusion ([43842380](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43842380)): boxed: "Sounds to me like they run the same experiment many times and keep the 'best' results. Which is cheating, or if the same thing is done in biomedical research: research fraud." ([43843679](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43843679))
- StevenWaterman explains why privacy alone doesn't fix it: "even if the benchmarks are private, it's still an issue. Because you can overfit to the benchmark by trying X random variations of the model, and picking the one that performs best... It's similar to how I can pass any multiple-choice exam if you let me keep attempting it." ([43844137](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43844137))
- gwern's judgment of arena voters: LMArena "can be easily gamed. The users are self-selected, and they have zero incentive to be honest or rigorous... either they're basically fraudulent, or LMArena's current users are people whose ratings you should be optimizing against because they are so ignorant, lazy, and superficial." ([43623704](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43623704))
- reissbaker: "the Chatbot Arena leaderboards aren't considered super-reliable benchmarks anymore, especially since they allow model makers to effectively hill-climb on them by pre-releasing models." ([43086794](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43086794))
- lhl had flagged the same dynamic a year earlier re: Gemma 2 training on arena data: "by optimizing for leaderboard scoring, it makes the leaderboard ranking less useful as a benchmark (Goodhart's law strikes again)." ([40848938](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40848938))

### 1.5 Benchmark-owner conflicts of interest — the FrontierMath/OpenAI affair

The January 2025 revelation that OpenAI funded FrontierMath and had access to most of the problem set — under a contract that forbade Epoch from disclosing it — is HN's canonical case of benchmark-institution capture ([42763231](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42763231), 483 points).

- benterix: "'Our contract specifically prevented us from disclosing information about the funding source and the fact that OpenAI has data access'... Man, this is huge." ([42767018](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42767018))
- Certhas frames the integrity test: "If what they care about is benchmark integrity, what reason would they have for demanding access to the benchmark dataset and hiding the fact that they finance it? The obvious thing to do if integrity is your goal is to fund it, declare that you will not touch it, and be transparent about it." ([42767697](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42767697))
- saithound: "it looks all but certain that OpenAI gamed this particular benchmark. Otherwise, they would not have had a contract that prohibited revealing that OpenAI was involved... until after the o3 announcements were made and the market had time to react." ([42766678](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42766678))
- optimalsolver, on the "held-out" claim: "everyone thought it was all locked up in a vault at Epoch AI HQ, but looks like Sam Altman has a copy on his bedside table." ([42767213](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42767213)) And: "There's absolutely no comeuppance for juicing benchmarks, especially ones no one has access to." ([42767415](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42767415))
- Imnimo widens it beyond the score: "What's much more concerning to me than the integrity of the benchmark number is the general pattern of behavior... We shouldn't accept secretly (even secret to the people doing the creation!) funding the creation of a benchmark. I also don't see how we can trust in the integrity of EpochAI going forward." ([42763649](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42763649))
- The dispute over "validation isn't training" (EvgeniyZh vs refulgentis, [42767027](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42767027)) shows HN treating soft leakage (using an eval as a dev/validation set) as cheating too.
- Related: Humanity's Last Exam contributors surfaced pay/authorship grievances from inside the pipeline (jbenoit: "Most of my submissions are being included in the benchmark, but I'm only getting paid $500 for one of them", [42807803](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42807803)) — the benchmark supply chain itself is seen as ad-hoc and misaligned.

### 1.6 Launch-day marketing crimes: tuned models, cherry-picked comparisons, chart fraud, undisclosed sampling tricks

Model announcements are read as adversarial marketing documents. Recurring sub-complaints: "tuned" asterisks, cons@64 vs pass@1 apples-to-oranges, omitted competitors, and misleading bar charts.

- o3/ARC-AGI (Dec 2024, [42473321](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42473321)): prng2021: "aren't these comparisons completely nonsense considering it's o3 tuned vs other non-tuned? ... I see a model doing great in another AI test but about to abysmally fail a variation of it that will come out soon." ([42476917](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42476917)); Bjorkbat: "if you look closely at the first sentence of the announcement they mention that they used a version of o3 trained on a public dataset of ARC-AGI, so technically it doesn't belong on this list" ([42475326](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42475326)); bluecoconut: "~=$3400 per single task to meet human performance on this benchmark is a lot... 'ARC-AGI-TUNED' makes me think they did some undisclosed amount of fine-tuning" ([42473876](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42473876)).
- GPT-5 launch (Aug 2025, [44826997](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44826997)): nabla9: "Holy misleading graph Batman/Altman! Academic benchmark score improves only 5% but they make the bar 50% higher." ([44827530](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44827530)); data-ottawa: "my first experience with GPT-5 is a presentation with incorrect charts and a game that looks shiny but has serious flaws" ([44836429](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44836429)); jennyholzer: "I do not believe that Open AI's benchmarks are legitimate and I don't think they have been for quite some time, if ever." ([44836386](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44836386))
- Sampling-trick disclosure: o1's AIME numbers mixed pass@1, "consensus among 64 samples," and re-ranking of 1000 samples (bluecoconut, [41524693](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41524693)); famouswaffles notes FrontierMath scores were quoted "one with pass@1 and another with consensus vote with 64 samples" ([42490049](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42490049)); tucnak on Grok 3: "doing incredibly well on human preference (LM Arena) however with Style Control, it's suddenly behind ChatGPT-4o-latest" ([43089225](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43089225)).
- Selective baselines: mkl on Llama 4: "the blog post didn't mention Gemini 2.5 at all... maybe the results make Llama 4 look worse?" - Self-scored wins: m3kw9 on "GLM 5.2 beats Claude in our benchmarks": "There is 2 suspicious words: 'Beats' and 'our benchmarks'" ([48719088](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48719088)); m3kw9 again: "Open source models showing a benchmark with any sort of 'Lead's is pure marketing." ### 1.7 The benchmarks themselves are broken: leaked answers, wrong labels, exploitable harnesses

A steady drumbeat of stories showing the measurement instruments are defective, independent of gaming:

- SWE-bench solution leakage ("Some critical issues with the SWE-bench dataset", [43130732](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43130732)): the paper's "32.67% of the successful patches involve cheating as the solutions were directly provided in the issue report or the comments" was widely quoted; modeless: "When we filtered out these problematic issues, the resolution rate of SWE-Agent+GPT-4 dropped from 12.47% to 3.97%. This matches my intuition... I don't think any current coding benchmark accurately measures coding performance." ([43131375](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43131375))
- SWE-bench git-history leaks (Sep 2025, [45214670](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45214670)): agents looked up the actual fix commit in the repo's future git history. SWE-bench team member lieret confirms: "it only seems to affect more recent models, maybe the result of reward hacking during posttraining" ([45217083](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45217083)). Tanjreeve: "If the benchmarks are just testing the ability to get the answers from history then something is clearly wrong with the benchmark... That's not the customers problem to solve on behalf of the vendor." ([45229488](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45229488))
- SWE-bench Verified post-mortem (Apr 2026, [47910388](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47910388)): gwd's summary of why scores stopped meaning anything: "A large number of the tests are inaccurate... Frontier models have already read and memorized the PRs the problems are based on... many problems are essentially impossible to get right if you haven't memorized the solution: the test cases will fail if you didn't happen to expose a helper function with a specific name... frontier models are passing that test anyway because they remember." ([47919382](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47919382))
- Answer keys are wrong: "About 30% of Humanity's Last Exam chemistry/biology answers are likely wrong" ([44668514](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44668514)); pixl97: "Quite often when a benchmark has a lot of questions it's eventually determined that some percentage of the questions is bad or completely wrong." ([49204536](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49204536))
- Agent harnesses are exploitable ("Exploiting the most prominent AI agent benchmarks", Apr 2026, [47733217](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47733217)): e.g. "Navigating Chromium to a file:// URL reads the gold answer directly from the task config — giving ~100% on all 812 WebArena tasks." kommunicate (infra operator): "It takes a ton of time and effort setting up benchmarks to run at scale with protection against reward hacks... You need to keep the grading script and the solution off the box." ([47755953](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47755953)) mrifaki: "the fix is the same one the RL community has been working on for years: make the verifier harder to game than the task is to solve; this paper shows that right now for most of these benchmarks the opposite is true." ([47747285](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47747285))
- Even the criticism papers get checked and found sloppy: comex re-derives two SWE-bench+ examples and finds "Some of the examples in the paper seem to be wrong" ([43132826](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43132826)) — HN applies the same adversarial audit to benchmark critics.

### 1.8 Saturation and the treadmill: "every new model saturates everything," then a new benchmark is minted

- ben_w, already in Jan 2025: "It's good to have a better test (though I bet this one will also be quickly saturated like all the others)." ([42566105](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42566105))
- jsnell's taxonomy: "We only have three types of benchmark: a) ones that have been saturated, b) ones where AI performance is progressing rapidly, c) really newly introduced ones that were specifically designed for the then-current frontier models to fail on." ([45003339](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45003339))
- osti on GPT-5.6's headline number: "it's an almost saturated benchmark where even some subpar open source models score very well... most models are clustered within a small range so it really doesn't tell you much." ([48849386](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48849386))
- seizethecheese's hard-won lesson: "No matter how hard I tried, I could not get any lift [on GPQA Diamond]. When Fable 5 dropped, it also did not improve upon Opus, and I realized my mistake. The benchmark was saturated!" ([49289059](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49289059))
- _ache_ gives the community heuristic: "Most values sitting @>75% in a benchmark generally indicate that it's no longer as useful as a <70% one." ([49310427](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49310427))
- wongarsu on the lifecycle: "Benchmarks are useful when they are first introduced and used to measure things that were released before the benchmark. After that their usefulness rapidly declines." ([43625618](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43625618))
- The treadmill is also invoked *against* skeptics: saturation-resistant series (METR time horizons, Epoch capability index) get cited as the only trustworthy longitudinal signal (aspenmartin, [48482807](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48482807)) — and dismissed in turn as "meaningless benchmarks, corporate marketing copy" (wizzwizz4, [46265439](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46265439)).

### 1.9 Scores don't transfer to real work — the vibes gap

Possibly the most broadly held position: whatever benchmarks measure, it isn't what users experience. Claude's years of mediocre benchmark scores vs. its reputation as the best coding model is the stock example.

- perrygeo: "Claude scores modestly on these benchmarks but vastly better than the alternatives in practice... The IRL performance advantage is not reflected in any of these benchmarks." ([43132985](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43132985))
- bearjaws: "OAI, xAI, Anthropic, Google all score incredibly well, then you go to try and write code and it's just okay. They claim it can do PHD level reasoning, but here I am not trusting it on basic computational thinking." ([43131922](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43131922))
- HarHarVeryFunny, full nihilism: "Benchmarks are meaningless. Try it on your own problems and see if it has improved for what you want to use it for." ([47794812](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47794812))
- darkoob12: "Benchmarks are meaningless. You can beat any model on any benchmark with the right training." ([48944505](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48944505))
- chrsw: "I don't even look at benchmarks anymore. I just try different models as they're released on our large, proprietary, systems software codebases... everything else is just noise." ([48703223](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48703223))
- neya wants the question reframed: "They should answer 'How does this model perform for MY use-case?' rather than 'How does this model perform across all coding environments?'" (Elixir vs Python example, [48714437](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48714437))
- "Many SWE-bench-Passing PRs would not be merged" (Mar 2026, [47341645](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47341645)) supplied the formal version: passing tests ≠ mergeable code (xthunk: "passing doesn't automatically mean it is maintainable", [47344701](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47344701)).
- mtrifonov argues even a perfect benchmark measures the wrong axis now: "SWE-bench is shaped like a one-shot problem set and frontier coding work isn't shaped like that anymore... Even a perfectly contamination-free benchmark would mostly test the wrong axis." ([47918258](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47918258))

### 1.10 Fragility under perturbation — memorization exposed by trivial variation

Directly relevant to PERTURB-Bench: HN treats "score collapses when you rename variables" as the smoking gun for memorization-not-reasoning.

- The headline thread: "30% drop in o1-preview accuracy when Putnam problems are slightly variated" (Jan 2025, [42565606](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565606)). yifanl: "Is it just an open secret that the models are currently just being hardcoded for random benchmarks?" ([42565744](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565744)); rrr_oh_man: "feels very much like students last-minute cramming for Asian style exams. The ability to perfectly regurgitate, while no concept of meaning." ([42565785](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565785)); itfossil: "they basically cheated and lied. If you are shocked by this, you are the sucker in the room." ([42565793](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565793))
- Important methodological caveat raised in the same thread — golol: "without a human control it is not at all clear to me that the variation problems are not more difficult." ([42565849](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565849))
- BoorishBears states perturbation as the standard contamination test: "the standard for quickly testing for benchmark contamination has always been to switch out specifics of the task. Like replacing named concepts with nonsense words in reasoning benchmarks." ([45970701](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45970701))
- mjr00's modified-riddle demo: give a trick-question *shape* without the trick and the model answers the memorized riddle ("the interviewer is the candidate's mother") — "clearly pattern matching and overfitting... no actual reasoning going on." ([45276358](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45276358))
- ARC-AGI-2 as a perturbation event: noosphr: "a version of o1 dropped from 32 percent on the original puzzles to just 3 percent on the new version... Arc AGI is the main reason why I don't trust static benchmarks." ([43588154](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43588154))
- Community-curated perturbation suites exist: WiSaGaN points to MisguidedAttention, "a curated benchmark just for those famous problems slightly variated." ([42565874](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565874))

### 1.11 Benchmarks as marketing artifacts / hype infrastructure — and selective belief

- SlinkyOnStairs (POSIWID): "AI companies have had years now to stop using unreliable benchmarks as advertising material. ([47542199](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47542199))

### 1.12 Retreat to vibes, private evals, and mascot benchmarks — with full awareness those get gamed too

The community's practical response is to stop trusting public numbers and run personal/private tests — while acknowledging the same Goodhart dynamics chase them there.

- pongogogo on Leaderboard Illusion: "at this point in time you can't trust any public benchmark, and you really need your own private evals." ([43843446](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43843446))
- mark_l_watson (2024, early statement of the norm): "For model comparison I have my own standard little tests that are my own and private, and I can be sure models are not tuned specifically for." ([40131605](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40131605))
- girvo: "I use benchmarks… that are my own private internal suite on my own code bases where I can judge the output properly." ([48837586](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48837586))

## 2. What HN says (or implies) would make a science benchmark trustworthy / flagship-grade

Synthesized from explicit proposals and from the negations of the complaints above. Items marked (E) are explicit community proposals; (I) are implied by complaints.

1. **Held-out, genuinely private test sets, ideally third-party administered.** (E) "The solution moving forward has to be private benchmark suites... It's so vital that it's not leaked" (perrygeo, [43132985](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43132985)). brookst's split: "You do it by releasing 90% of the benchmark publicly and holding back 10% for yourself or closely trusted partners. Then benchmark performance can be independently evaluated to determine if performance on the 10% holdback matches the 90% public." ([44536077](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44536077)). ARC-AGI's semi-private eval set is repeatedly cited as the working model (cwyers, [45756268](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45756268)). ghaff (pre-LLM, on crash tests): a third-party org that runs the test and gives you one shot per generation — while noting the cost: "you're now benchmarking with a totally opaque test that you have to trust" ([23693144](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23693144)).
2. **Rotation / liveness: refresh the test set on a schedule, post-training-cutoff.** (E) astro1234: "Private, refreshed test sets attack the mechanism itself, and in my view they are the only intervention that does. If the questions have never touched the public Web, they can't be in the training data; if they rotate, memorizing this year's set doesn't help next year." ([49188106](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49188106)). semi-extrinsic's quarterly versioned crowdsourced dataset: "People can then only evaluate their coding LLM on datasets published after their training period." ([43131464](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43131464)). The Konwinski Prize's freeze-then-scrape design ("scraping a new test set after submissions are frozen... guaranteed that agents can't 'cheat'", andyk, [42435555](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42435555)) and LiveBench are the named exemplars.
3. **Perturbation robustness as a first-class, reported metric.** (E) Variable renaming, constant changes, nonsense-word substitution, noise injection — and publish the delta. dannyw's prescription for a serious private eval: "add things you care about, such as reliability (e.g. deliberate noise injection, simple typo introduction in problems, variants, running each test multiple times)." ([47918318](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47918318)). BoorishBears: swapping task specifics "has always been" the contamination test ([45970701](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45970701)). The Putnam-AXIOM 30%-drop result is the community's proof this works. Caveat from golol: perturbed variants need human/difficulty controls so the drop can't be dismissed as "the variants are just harder" ([42565849](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42565849)).
4. **Rate-limited, audited submission — defend against adaptive overfitting, not just training leakage.** (E) StevenWaterman: private isn't enough if vendors can query repeatedly and select the best variant ([43844137](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43844137)); amelius proposes hard submission quotas ("1 model per company per month, max", [43846623](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43846623)). The Leaderboard Illusion complaints (private variant testing, retractable scores) define the anti-pattern.
5. **Independence: no lab funding secrecy, no privileged data access, disclosed conflicts.** (I/E) The FrontierMath standard, per Certhas: fund it if you must, "declare that you will not touch it, and be transparent about it" ([42767697](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42767697)). No NDAs concealing sponsor access; no "verbal agreements" in place of enforceable blinding (aithrowawaycomm, [42766769](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42766769)).
6. **Verified answer keys, published error rates, versioned/immutable datasets.** (I) The HLE ~30%-wrong-answers story and SWE-bench Verified's flawed tests are the cautionary tales; ukFxqnLa2sBSBf6's questions — did anyone review the items? are they locked for reproducibility? ([43131799](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43131799)) — read as a minimal QA checklist. pixl97: assume some fraction of items is wrong until independently audited ([49204536](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49204536)).
7. **Hard-to-game task design: hard to solve, easy to verify, no recoverable ground truth.** (E) optimalsolver's three properties: "1) No known solutions, so there's no 'ground truth' dataset to train on. 2) Presumably hard to solve. 3) But easy to verify a solution if one is provided." ([43131670](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43131670)). mrifaki's rule for agentic evals: "make the verifier harder to game than the task is to solve." ([47747285](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47747285)). Sandboxed harnesses with grading scripts and solutions off-box (kommunicate, [47755953](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47755953)); no gold answers reachable from the environment; no .git history containing the fix (gwd's SWE-bench list).
8. **Apples-to-apples, fully disclosed conditions.** (I) No "tuned"-vs-untuned rows on one chart (o3/ARC); pass@1 vs cons@64 vs re-ranked clearly labeled (o1 AIME); compute/cost per task reported (bluecoconut's $3400/task, [42473876](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42473876)); honest axes on charts (nabla9). Style-control-style corrections where presentation confounds substance (tucnak, [43089225](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43089225)).
9. **Human baselines, including on the perturbed/refreshed variants.** (I) ARC's "smart human >95%" framing is what made o3's asterisks legible; golol's control objection; pehtran: a credible test is one a "diverse set of human judges" would pass judgment on ([48957066](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48957066)).
10. **Open artifacts: attributable leaderboard entries, published transcripts/trajectories, outside replication.** (E) cwyers praises SWE-bench because "anyone can run it and [there's] an open leaderboard where they attribute the results to specific models" ([45756268](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45756268)); SWE-bench's lieret responds to the git-leak scandal by building public trajectory inspection tools "to get even more eyes on the trajectories" ([45217083](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45217083)); __alexs's quip to a private-eval boaster: "Publishing them might help you find out." ([43848049](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43848049))
11. **Statistical honesty: error bars, adequate sample sizes, saturation reporting.** (I) yorwba on measurement noise ([47542199](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47542199)); _ache_'s saturation heuristic (>75% clustering = dead benchmark, [49310427](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49310427)); jeremyh1's complaint that binary pass/fail scoring "does not capture" capability gaps ([49006193](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49006193)).
12. **Measure real work, not proxy puzzles.** (E/I) Tasks drawn from production work (Ramp's "private contamination-free benchmark from production work", [48507641](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48507641)); mergeability/maintainability judgments, not just tests passing (the Mar 2026 SWE-bench PR study); wall-clock time and cost as reported dimensions (girvo, [48837586](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48837586)).
13. **Anti-Goodhart humility: plan for your own gaming and retirement.** (I) mrandish: assume an adversarial environment, budget for measure/counter-measure forever ([46817735](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46817735)); wongarsu's lifecycle (usefulness decays after release); expect labs to treat your benchmark as a training target the day it ships (davidgerard, [42824260](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42824260)).

The composite HN ideal, stated once: xianshou on the Konwinski Prize — "more unbiased evals with known methodology but hidden tests are exactly what we need... I trust small but well-curated benchmarks... over large, widely targeted, and increasingly saturated or gamed benchmarks... Goodhart's law is thriving and it's our duty to fight it." ([42432731](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42432731))
